Packet capture for your pocket

See what your phone is saying to the network.

Pcapy turns your phone into a packet-capture probe. It records the traffic of the apps you pick and streams it live into Wireshark on your computer, with the app name beside every packet.

Install the Wireshark plugin {{> stores}}

How it works

  1. Pick your appsChoose which apps to watch and set a password. Pcapy runs as a local VPN on the phone, so nothing has to be configured on the network.
  2. Start the captureYour apps keep working normally while Pcapy records their packets to a pcapng file and offers a live stream on your local network.
  3. Watch it in WiresharkPick the Pcapy: phone interface, enter the phone's address and your password, and packets appear live.

What you get

Per-app capture

On Android, capture only the apps you select. The rest of the phone stays out of the file.

App names in Wireshark

Every packet is tagged with the app that sent it. Filter with pcapy.app == "com.example.app" or add an App column.

Live, password-protected stream

Wireshark connects with a password you choose. The password itself is never sent, and repeated wrong guesses lock the address out.

DNS you can see

Lookups are kept readable, so you can tell which hosts an app is talking to.

Saved captures

Every session is saved as a standard .pcapng file you can share or open in any tool.

Stays on your devices

No account, no ads, no analytics. Your captures go nowhere you don't send them. Read the privacy policy.

Good to know

No decryption. HTTPS stays encrypted. You see who talked to whom, when and how much, plus the app name on Android, but not the contents.

The live stream is not encrypted. The password stops casual connections, but anyone on the same Wi-Fi can read the packets in transit. Use a network you trust.

Use Pcapy only on devices and networks you own or are authorised to test.
